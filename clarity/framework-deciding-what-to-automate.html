A Simple Framework for Deciding What to Automate First

August 11, 2026

Every small business has a list of things that feel like they should be automated. Almost none of them have the budget to automate all of it at once. Here's how to actually pick.

Ask what's costing you time, not what's annoying

Annoying and expensive aren't the same thing. A five-minute task that irritates you once a month costs far less than a tedious fifteen-minute task someone does every single day. Automate based on actual hours recovered, not based on what bothers you most in the moment.

The "twice a week" test

A rough but genuinely useful filter: if a task happens twice a week or more, it's usually worth automating. Below that frequency, the time and cost of building the automation often outweighs what you'd actually save, at least as a first priority.

Start with the smallest version that helps

You don't need to automate an entire process end to end on the first attempt. Automating just the worst, most repetitive step inside a larger process often delivers most of the relief, for a fraction of the cost and risk of automating the whole thing at once.

What NOT to automate first

Anything that's still actively changing, a process you're not sure is even the right one yet, is a bad first candidate. Automating a broken or unsettled process just makes it faster to do the wrong thing. Get the process right first, then automate it.

A short audit is often the fastest way to get an outside, honest read on where your first dollar should actually go.
